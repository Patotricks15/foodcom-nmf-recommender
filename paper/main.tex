\begin{abstract}
We evaluate a from-scratch, multiplicative-update Non-negative Matrix
Factorization (NMF) for top-$N$ Food.com recipe recommendation under a
strict global temporal split, combined with a post-hoc nutrition-aware
filter over calories, sodium, and allergens. Unlike signed factorizations
or pairwise-ranking embeddings, NMF constrains both factor matrices to be
non-negative, yielding additive, interpretable latent components. The
system includes point-in-time features, strong non-neural baselines,
reproducible Floci/AWS infrastructure, and a measurement of how much the
nutrition filter changes the ranking.
\end{abstract}
