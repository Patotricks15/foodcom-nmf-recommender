ItemKNN is strongest on the fixture; NMF substantially improves over the
popularity floor but does not beat the tuned neighbourhood method on raw
ranking quality. This negative result is retained deliberately: the point
of this portfolio entry is interpretability and a principled nutrition
constraint, not a claim that NMF dominates every classical baseline.

The nutrition filter removes the large majority of the 50-recipe candidate
pool under the (realistic, moderately strict) 450-calorie/600-mg-sodium
constraint used here, and the resulting NDCG@10 drop quantifies the cost of
enforcing that constraint rather than leaving it hidden inside an opaque
re-ranking step. A looser constraint removes fewer candidates and costs
less ranking quality; both numbers are reported by
\texttt{scripts/export\_results\_table.py} for any constraint setting, so
the trade-off is inspectable rather than asserted.

\section{Deployment}
Training writes factors and feature tables to S3 and publishes precomputed,
already nutrition-filtered top-$N$ lists to DynamoDB. Lambda performs a
bounded key lookup. EventBridge schedules nightly retraining and CloudWatch
receives logs. The default Terraform profile targets the local Floci AWS
emulator and therefore requires no paid AWS account.
